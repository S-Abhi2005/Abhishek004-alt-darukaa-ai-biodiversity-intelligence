# IPBES Pollinator Habitat Connectivity & Landscape Heterogeneity

## Drivers of Pollinator Decline in Agricultural Landscapes
The IPBES Global Thematic Assessment highlights that >40% of invertebrate pollinator species face extinction due to habitat fragmentation, agrochemical exposure, landscape homogenization, and seasonal floral deserts created by large-scale monocultures.

## Habitat Corridors and Stepping Stones
- **Max Flight Distance Constraints**: Small-bodied solitary bees (e.g., *Andrena*, *Lasioglossum*) have restricted foraging ranges of 100-300 meters from nest sites. Bombus (bumblebees) can forage up to 800-1,500m.
- **Continuous Stepping-Stone Corridors**: Establishing non-crop floral refugia every 200-250 meters connects isolated fragmented patches, sustaining genetic interchange and colony survival.

## Phenological Sequence Requirements
- **Continuous Nectar & Pollen Flow**: Plant communities must guarantee overlapping bloom periods across early spring, mid-summer, and late autumn.
- **Floral Diversity Standard**: Minimum 8-12 entomophilous native flowering species representing at least 3 distinct plant families (e.g., Fabaceae, Asteraceae, Lamiaceae, Rosaceae).

## Economic Yield Elasticity
- Over 75% of leading global food crop types rely on animal pollination.
- Landscape-level pollinator strips enhance adjacent fruit and oilseed set by 18-32%, reducing yield variation caused by climatic fluctuations.
