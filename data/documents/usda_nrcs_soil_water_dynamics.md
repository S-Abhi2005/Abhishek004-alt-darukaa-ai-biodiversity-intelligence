# USDA NRCS Soil Water Dynamics & Infiltration Mechanics

## Available Water Capacity (AWC) and Organic Matter
- **The 1% Rule**: USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) empirical models indicate that every 1% absolute increase in Soil Organic Matter (SOM) in the upper 15 cm of soil profile increases Available Water Capacity (AWC) by approximately 20,000 to 27,000 gallons per acre (~187,000 to 252,000 liters per hectare).
- **Pore Size Distribution**: SOM addition improves the ratio of meso-pores (responsible for plant-available water storage) to macro-pores (drainage) and micro-pores (water bound at permanent wilting point).

## Bulk Density and Compaction Alleviation
- **Threshold of Root Restriction**:
  - Sandy soils: Bulk density > 1.60 g/$cm^3$ restricts root penetration.
  - Silty/Loamy soils: Bulk density > 1.40 g/$cm^3$ restricts root penetration.
  - Heavy Clay soils: Bulk density > 1.25 g/$cm^3$ restricts root penetration.
- **Remediation**: Deep-taproot cover crops (Daikon Radish / *Raphanus sativus var. longipinnatus*, Forage Brassicas) exert bio-drilling forces exceeding 2.0 MPa, puncturing plow-pan layers without subsoiling horsepower requirements.

## Infiltration Rate Dynamics
- In conventionally tilled degraded soils, infiltration rates regularly drop below 5-10 mm/hour, precipitating rapid sheet erosion during rainfall events exceeding 15 mm/hr.
- Bio-remediated soils featuring undisturbed biopores and organic mulch support sustained infiltration rates of 45-120 mm/hour.
