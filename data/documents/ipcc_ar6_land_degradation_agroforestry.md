# IPCC AR6 Land Degradation & Multi-Strata Agroforestry

## Land Degradation Neutrality (LDN) Mechanisms
According to the IPCC Special Report on Climate Change and Land (SRCCL) and AR6 WGIII Chapter 7, land degradation affects 1.5 to 3.2 billion people globally. Agroforestry is ranked as one of the highest mitigation and adaptation co-benefit options, with economic mitigation potential exceeding 1.1-2.6 Gt $CO_2e$/year at costs below $50/t $CO_2e$.

## Multi-Strata Microclimate Buffering
- **Canopy Temperature Attenuation**: Mature tree canopies reduce understory peak summer soil temperatures by 3.5°C to 7.2°C, suppressing heat-induced leaf senescence and flower abortion in staple crops (coffee, cocoa, maize).
- **Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)**: Tree shelterbelts and shade trees depress boundary layer VPD by 0.6-1.4 kPa, substantially reducing transpirational stress during drought spells.

## Deep Soil Organic Carbon & Root Architecture
- Tree root systems penetrate subsoils (>1.0m to 4.0m), depositing recalcitrant suberin and lignin-rich root exudates directly into mineral horizons.
- Deep root channels act as preferential conduits for winter groundwater recharge while preventing hydraulic lifting of saline tables when managed with selective pruning.

## Slope Stability and Erosion Control
- On slopes >8%, contour agroforestry (alley cropping with leguminous hedges like *Gliricidia sepium* or *Calliandra calothyrsus*) cuts topsoil erosion by 60-95%, while reducing downhill sediment transport.
