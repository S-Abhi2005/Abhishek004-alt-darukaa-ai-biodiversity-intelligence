# FAO Integrated Pest Management & Multi-Tier Riparian Buffers

## Ecosystem Service Architecture of Riparian Buffers
Vegetated riparian buffer strips serve as semi-natural ecotones between agricultural production fields and aquatic freshwater networks. When designed as multi-tier zones (trees, perennial shrubs, deep-rooted graminoids), they deliver dual functions: hydrological filtration and biological pest suppression.

## Chemical and Sediment Filtration Dynamics
- **Particulate Interception**: Dense native grass zones (*Vetiveria zizanioides*, *Panicum virgatum*) arrest 70-90% of overland sediment wash-off from slopes up to 12%.
- **Nitrate Removal ($NO_3^-$)**: Saturated sub-surface root zones promote anaerobic microbial denitrification ($2 NO_3^- \rightarrow N_2$), stripping 65-85% of dissolved agricultural nitrogen before entering water tables.
- **Pesticide Degradation**: Plant exudates and rhizosphere mycorrhizal fungi accelerate hydrophobic pesticide binding ($K_{oc}$) and organophosphate microbial catabolism.

## Biocontrol and Functional Agrobiodiversity
- Multi-tier buffers harbor predatory arthropods (Carabidae, Staphylinidae, spiders) and parasitoid wasps (Ichneumonidae, Braconidae).
- Fields flanked by 8-15m floral riparian strips demonstrate a 34-52% drop in aphid and lepidopteran pest populations, lowering chemical insecticide requirements below economic injury thresholds (EIT).

## Quantitative Design Parameters
- **Minimum Width**: 5 meters for basic sediment trapping; 12-25 meters for comprehensive pesticide/nitrate filtration and avian corridor function.
- **Slope Adjustments**: Add 1 meter of buffer width per 1% increase in slope above 5%.
- **Vegetative Composition**: 40% native high-canopy trees, 35% fruiting/nectariferous shrubs, 25% perennial bunchgrasses.
